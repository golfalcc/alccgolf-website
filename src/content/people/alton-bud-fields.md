Alton "Bud" Fields was one of the most accomplished golfers in the
history of the Mount Union course. Club records credit him with **14
men's club championships** between 1945 and 1971, a span of twenty-six
years that connected the club's early postwar era to a new generation of
local competitors.

His championship years were **1945, 1947, 1949, 1951, 1953, 1954,
1955, 1958, 1959, 1963, 1964, 1965, 1970, and 1971**. Only Elinor Wible's
extraordinary record of 48 women's titles exceeds his total in the
club's surviving championship lists.

## A Golfer Who Knew the Course

Fields's record was built on intimate knowledge of the Mount Union
layout and a short game that repeatedly separated him from strong
fields. In July 1947, *The Daily News* reported that his 73 in a Central
Counties match against Blairmont established a course record. The paper
also described him as a longtime member of the Mount Union team and a
constant worker on the course.

He lowered the amateur course record again in July 1950, shooting a
five-under-par 70 during a putting tournament. The professional record
at the time was reported as 68.

## The First Mount Union Open

Fields won the Mount Union Open on July 6, 1952, with a three-under-par
71. The tournament brought professionals and leading amateurs to Mount
Union, but the hometown player finished one stroke ahead of professional
Henry Lyons and amateurs Dave Wallace and John Walter, who tied at 72.

The newspaper's account emphasized that Fields's short game made the
difference. His victory earned the tournament's top amateur merchandise
prize and established him as the first winner of an event that became a
major part of Mount Union's golf calendar.

## Club Championships

Fields won his first surviving club title in 1945 and added another in
1947. In the 1949 final, he defeated Harry Cholewinski 8 and 7 in a
36-hole match. He continued winning through the 1950s, collecting eight
club championships by the time he captured the Central Counties title
in 1959.

His twelfth club championship came in 1965. That victory was his third
consecutive title, and he defeated Don Dimoff 5 and 4 in the 36-hole
final. Contemporary coverage reported a field of 96 golfers across six
flights. Fields later returned to win the championship in 1970 and 1971,
bringing his documented total to fourteen.

## Central Counties Golf

Fields was also one of Mount Union's leading representatives in Central
Counties competition. He earned medalist honors at the 1958 Central
Counties championship on his home course with a two-under-par 72. The
following year, at Lewistown Country Club, he defeated former Penn State
captain Rod Eaken on the eighteenth hole to win the 33rd Central
Counties Amateur Golf Tournament.

These performances show that Fields's success extended well beyond club
championship play. He could qualify at the front of large regional
fields and win match-play titles away from home.

## Family Recollection and Photographs

Fields's son, Charles "Charlie" Fields, has helped identify surviving
club photographs. In a firsthand written exchange shared with the club
in 2026, Charles identified one image as Bud teeing off from the old
seventeenth tee. He also confirmed that another photograph showed Bud
with Charles's mother and Nan Anderson. Those identifications preserve
valuable context that the photographs themselves did not record.

## Legacy

Bud Fields's fourteen club championships, course-record rounds, Mount
Union Open victory, and Central Counties title place him among the most
important competitive golfers in ALCC history. His record is not a
single great season but more than a quarter-century of success on the
same course he knew, represented, and helped define.

For the complete year-by-year record, see [Men's Club Champions](/history/mens-club-champions).
