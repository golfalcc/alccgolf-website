Roy Paul Miller Davis was the principal promoter associated with the
Juniata Valley Colony Club's creation. On August 26, 1924, he helped
convene the organizing meeting at Mount Union High School. A contemporary
report counted 128 stockholders and charter members and described a plan
that joined a country club to a summer-cottage development along the
Juniata River.

The undertaking was larger than golf. Davis promoted cottage lots, rental
homes, roads, water and sewer service, a clubhouse, swimming, tennis,
boating, fishing, and trapshooting. His August 1926 prospectus described
roughly 600 acres and explained how land sales, visitors, rentals, and club
dues were expected to support the development. Those figures preserve the
promoter's ambitions; they do not prove that every proposed cottage or lot
was completed.

Outside the club, Davis was a Princeton graduate, banker, manufacturer,
and prolific inventor. His best-known products included the Sta-Neet
razor-and-comb combination.

## A Complicated Financial Record

Davis's role also places him at the center of the project's financial
story. In a December 1929 public letter, he carefully distinguished the
Juniata Valley Realty Corporation, which developed and owned land, from
the Colony Club, which operated the recreational institution. He discussed
borrowing, credit pressure, and bankruptcy threats while defending both
organizations. His letter is important evidence of his position, but it
was not an independent financial audit.

The project continued to plan expansion in 1930 even as stockholders
authorized a proposed increase in indebtedness. This tension is essential
to understanding the Colony Club: Davis helped create an unusually
ambitious regional recreation community, but the business behind it was
under strain years before the 1936 flood.
