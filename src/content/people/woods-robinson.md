Woods Robinson emerged from the club's own caddie ranks to become one of
the first great golfers produced by the Mount Union course. He won four
club championships—**1930, 1933, 1934, and 1935**—and established a
standard that endured well beyond his teenage years.

## From Caddie to Champion

Robinson began caddying at the course around age twelve. By 1930, he was
a fifteen-year-old sophomore at Mount Union High School and already
competing against the club's leading players.

That season, he defeated Carl "Jack" Kirk, the 1929 champion, in the
semifinal. Robinson then beat Melvin Smith **2 up with 1 hole to play** in
the final to capture his first club championship. A contemporary Mount
Union newspaper account celebrated the young champion under the headline
"Woods Robinson Wins Golf Trophy" and noted that he had also represented
the Mount Union club in team matches at Butch Hills, Lewistown, and the
Nittany Club at Bellefonte.

The report identified his father, A. M. Robinson, as the Pennsylvania
Railroad agent at Mount Union. It also described Woods as well known in
local school circles—a hometown student whose golf had developed on the
same course where he had carried bags only a few years earlier.

## The First Multiple Champion

Robinson returned to the championship final in 1933. In a 36-hole match,
he defeated defending champion Dick Fetterolf **4 and 2**. The surviving
account gives Robinson rounds of 79 and 79, compared with Fetterolf's 81
and 80, and identifies Robinson as the first member to win two Juniata
Valley club championships.

He did not stop at two. Robinson successfully added the 1934 and 1935
titles, giving him three consecutive championships and four victories in
six seasons. His total remains tied among the strongest championship
records in club history.

## Life at Juniata College

Robinson later attended Juniata College. The 1937 *Alfarata* yearbook
records him as its editor. In his editor's message, Robinson thanked the
staff and wrote of preserving the friendships and happiness of the
college year—a surviving glimpse of him beyond the golf course.

## Legacy

Woods Robinson's place in club history rests on more than four titles.
His story connects the early caddie program, Mount Union's schools, the
club's regional matches, and the formative years of its championship.
From a twelve-year-old caddie to a fifteen-year-old champion and the
first documented multiple winner, he was one of the course's earliest
homegrown stars.
